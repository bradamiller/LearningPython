## Turning until you face it

`turn_to(current_heading, desired_heading)` knows both headings. Each clockwise quarter
turn adds 1 to the heading, so the robot can simply turn clockwise while it isn't facing
the right way:

```python
# while current_heading is not desired_heading:
#     one clockwise quarter turn
#     current_heading goes up by 1, wrapping 3 back to 0
```

Adding 1 isn't enough on its own when the robot turns from West to North: 3 + 1 is 4, and
4 isn't a heading. The remainder operator `%` from Lesson 2-5 wraps it:

```python
heading = (heading + 1) % 4
```

For 1, 2 and 3 the remainder is the number itself, and `4 % 4` is 0, so after West comes
North. Trace the loop from South (2) to East (1):

| pass | heading before | heading after, `(h + 1) % 4` | facing East? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 (S) | 3 (W) | no |
| 2 | 3 (W) | 0 (N) | no |
| 3 | 0 (N) | 1 (E) | yes, the loop ends |

Three passes. The loop never asks which way is shorter: it always goes clockwise, so to
face what's a quarter turn to its left it turns three quarters of the way around. It works,
and it's simple; Lesson 4-6 has an optional challenge to make it quicker.

If the robot already faces the right way, the condition is False on the first check and
the loop does nothing, with no special case. At the end the function returns
`current_heading`, which by then equals `desired_heading`.

<TeacherNote title="The long way round is on purpose">

Get students standing and turning clockwise a quarter at a time, calling out 0, 1, 2, 3,
0: the kinesthetic bit cements the wrap. The loop always turns clockwise because it is
the simplest turn that always works. Students will spot the three-turn corners in the
printout; agree, and point them at the optional challenge in Lesson 4-6. Three
sensor-ended turns take three times as long as one and give the line sensors three
chances to miss.

An endless `turning clockwise` printout is the screen version of a robot spinning forever,
and much easier to stop and read. Usual causes: the heading isn't updated in the loop, or
`get_desired_heading()` returned `None`.

</TeacherNote>

## Wrap-up

- How does `turn_to()` know when to stop? (Its loop runs until the heading equals the one it wants; already facing it means zero turns.)
- Why `(heading + 1) % 4`? (Each clockwise turn adds 1, and `% 4` wraps 4 back to 0.)
- Why does the robot sometimes turn three times? (The loop always turns clockwise, so a quarter turn left takes three quarter turns right.)
