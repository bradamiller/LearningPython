## This lesson's skeleton

Paste this into `robot.py` above `RUN` — it lands at the end of the LINE TRACKING
section, which is where it belongs. The first real driving *function* goes in next to
the test that has been doing the job so far.

```python
# --- Lesson 2-8: follow to a cross ---
# track_to_intersection() is the follower from test_follow()
# with a reason to stop: it follows until is_at_cross() says
# the sensors are on a crossing line, then drives on until
# the robot's center is on it. Lesson 2-9 adds the turns.

def track_to_intersection():
    # until is_at_cross():
    #     correction = get_error() * KP
    #     steer with arcade(BASE_EFFORT, correction)
    # then stop the motors
    # then drive straight 8.75 cm: the robot's center onto
    # the cross
    pass

def test_cross_moving():
    # robot 20-30 cm from a straight line, square on: drive
    # until the cross check fires
    while not is_at_cross():
        drivetrain.set_effort(0.3, 0.3)
    drivetrain.stop()
    print("Cross!")

def test_track():
    # robot on the circle, pointed along it: follow to the
    # cross and stop
    track_to_intersection()
    print("Done!")

def test_reverse():
    # follow to the cross, turn around on it, follow back
    track_to_intersection()
    drivetrain.turn(180)
    track_to_intersection()
    print("Done!")
```

The loop's body is the body of `test_follow()`. What's new is the `while` line and the
drive after the loop.

## One more test, moving this time

Sliding the robot by hand showed that `is_at_cross()` reads the tape correctly. It
didn't show whether a *driving* robot gets a clean reading at the moment both sensors
arrive on the strip. `test_cross_moving()` checks that by itself, before any line
following is in the program: it's Lesson 2-2's drive-to-the-edge program with the
condition swapped for a function call. If the robot stops with both sensors on the
tape, `is_at_cross()` works at driving speed, and anything that goes wrong *later* is
not its fault. If it drives straight through, the sensors crossed the tape faster than
the loop read them — drop the effort to 0.2 — or the threshold is too high. If it
stops before the tape, something lighter than the floor counted as black.

## Follow until the cross

Now the follower. `test_follow()` is `while True:` — it follows forever. Change the
condition, and the *looking* is done by the loop condition while the *following* is
done inside the loop. The body is the two lines you already have, and the outline in
the skeleton says so.

Read the `while` line as a sentence: *while not at a cross, follow.* Nothing inside
the loop checks for the cross — that's the condition's job. Nothing in the condition
steers — that's the body's job. When both sensors reach the cross, `is_at_cross()`
returns `True`, the loop ends, and the robot stops.

## Arriving: the center on the cross

When the loop ends, the sensors are on the cross. But they sit at the very front of
the robot, and the robot turns about its **center of rotation**, the hole between the
drive wheels where a pen goes, 8.75 cm further back. So `track_to_intersection()` ends
with one more move: drive straight 8.75 cm. Now the robot's center is on the cross.
That does two jobs:

- **Turns spin on the cross.** Turn from where the sensors stopped and the robot swings
  about a point short of the cross. Turn from the center and the sensors come down on
  the new line.
- **The next track starts clean.** The sensors end up past the tape, so the next
  `track_to_intersection()` has a line to follow instead of stopping at once.

<Figure

  caption="Both robots stopped with their sensors on the cross. The left one turns right away and swings about a point short of it; the right one drives 8.75 cm first, so it spins on the cross and its sensors find the new line."

It's a distance, not a `time.sleep()`, on purpose. Eight and three-quarter centimeters
is the same with a full battery or a flat one; half a second is not. You know the
drivetrain call that drives a distance: you've used it since Lesson 1-8.

<TeacherNote title="Sensor-ended loops, and arriving with the center on the cross">

An earlier version of this lesson did the cross check and the following in one
`if/else` inside a `while True`, before the sensor functions existed. Students got it
working but couldn't say which half was broken when it wasn't. Now the sensor
functions arrive already tested, `is_at_cross()` gets one more test at driving speed,
and `track_to_intersection()` is `test_follow()`'s loop with the condition replaced.
Keep that separation visible — every function in Lesson 2-9 follows it, and it's why
the final project assembles rather than debugs.

The 8.75 cm drive is built into `track_to_intersection()` so it is taught once.
From here to Module 6, "track to the next intersection" means the robot is there,
center on the cross, ready to turn, and no later lesson repeats it. The 8.75 cm is
sensor-to-axle on an XRP; measure it if the robot's mounting differs. On tight curves
both sensors can briefly go high (a false positive); a wide, crisp taped cross and a
little tuning handle it.

</TeacherNote>

## Activity · Drive on the toolkit

The skeleton is pasted in at the end of the LINE TRACKING section; the run block still
waits for the button before every test.

### Step 1 · is_at_cross(), moving

Set the run call to `test_cross_moving()`. Robot 20 to 30 cm from a straight line,
square on. It should stop with both sensors on the tape. Try it from three distances.
Don't go on until it does.

### Step 2 · track_to_intersection()

Write the body — start by copying the loop out of `test_follow()` — and set the run
call to `test_track()`. Put the robot on the circle, pointed along it. It should follow the line around and stop with its center on the cross: put a finger on the tape beside the robot, and the pen hole between the wheels should sit over it. If it
weaves, this is the same gain you tuned in Lesson 2-6; if it stops somewhere that
isn't the cross, step 1 already told you the cross check is fine, so look at where the
follower put the sensors.

### Step 3 · Follow, reverse, follow

Set the run call to `test_reverse()`. The robot follows to the cross, turns around on
it, and follows the circle back the other way to the same cross.

### Step 4 · Challenge — count the crossings

Write `test_count()`: the robot reverses at the cross **four times** and stops,
printing the count at each cross — "Cross # 1", "Cross # 2", and so on, then "Done!".
You know a loop that runs a fixed number of times. This is a direct preview of the
Module 2 final project.

<Reveal title="Check your track_to_intersection()" hint="write it and test it on the circle first">

```python
def track_to_intersection():
    while not is_at_cross():
        correction = get_error() * KP
        drivetrain.arcade(BASE_EFFORT, correction)
    drivetrain.stop()
    drivetrain.straight(8.75, 0.5)
```

If yours reads the error a different way and passes the test, keep yours.

</Reveal>

# --- Lesson 2-8: follow to a cross ---
# track_to_intersection() is the follower from test_follow()

# the robot's center is on it. Lesson 2-9 adds the turns.

def track_to_intersection():
    while not is_at_cross():
        correction = get_error() * KP
        drivetrain.arcade(BASE_EFFORT, correction)
    drivetrain.stop()
    drivetrain.straight(8.75, 0.5)

def test_cross_moving():
    while not is_at_cross():
        drivetrain.set_effort(0.3, 0.3)
    drivetrain.stop()

def test_track():
    track_to_intersection()
    print("Done!")

def test_reverse():
    track_to_intersection()
    drivetrain.turn(180)
    track_to_intersection()
    print("Done!")

def test_count():
    for count in range(4):
        track_to_intersection()
        print("Cross #", count + 1)

board.wait_for_button()

## Wrap-up

- Why test `is_at_cross()` with a straight drive first? (So a failure can only be the cross check.)
- Where does `track_to_intersection()` look for the cross? (In the `while` condition; the body only steers.)
- What ends `track_to_intersection()`'s loop? (Both sensors on the cross — not a timer, not a count.)
- Why drive 8.75 cm after the loop? (The sensors reach the cross first; the robot's center, which it turns about, is 8.75 cm behind them.)
